Stones in the beans: grinder codes and burr damage

2026-10-26

Every super-automatic grinder has an enemy the error menu never names: the small stone that travels with raw coffee from the harvest. Two codes from two different brands point at it. On a Jura it is Error 12, "grinder failed to activate". On a De'Longhi it is the grinder-stuck message that newer models log as code 1454. Different electronics, same pebble.

Two codes, one cause

Jura Error 12 means the control board commanded a grind and the grinder never turned: the motor is seized or the burrs are impeded — most often by a stone picked up with the beans. The De'Longhi equivalent is a stalled grinder motor caused by a stone, a very oily bean layer, or grounds packed under the burr. De'Longhi shows words rather than numbers on most machines, so you may never see "1454" on the display, but that is the number the service menu logs.

Why harvest stones jam burrs

Coffee cherries are dried on patios and raised beds in the producing country, and small stones, clods and grit get mixed in with the beans. Roasters screen and sort, but a stone close to a roasted bean in size and weight occasionally slips through, and it does not float out with the defective beans.

The problem is geometry. At espresso fineness the gap between the two burrs is a fraction of a millimetre. A bean shears apart in that gap; a stone does not. It wedges, the motor stalls against it, and the board raises the code. Oily dark roasts cause the same stall a slower way: oils and fines cake underneath the burrs until the chamber is packed solid.

The GIGA wrinkle: which mill?

Two-grinder Jura GIGA machines have a left and a right grinder, and Error 12 names the left grinder specifically — useful, because it tells you exactly which hopper to empty and which throat to vacuum. One caveat from the service notes: on the GIGA 6 the same number can instead flag a communication or sensor fault, which a power cycle addresses first. So unplug, restart, and only then start treating it as a stone.

The inspection protocol

Work through this in order before concluding anything is broken.

  1. Unplug the machine for five minutes and restart — on the GIGA 6 this clears the communication-fault variant of Error 12.
  2. Empty the bean hopper completely. Inspect the beans from the bottom of the hopper for stones or clumped, stuck-together roasts; run them through your fingers rather than eyeballing them.
  3. Vacuum the hopper outlet and the grinder throat with a narrow nozzle. Never poke metal into the burrs — a screwdriver chips the edge you are trying to save.
  4. On a De'Longhi, turn the grind dial to its coarsest setting and back — with the grinder off is fine for this once. (Normally the dial should only be moved while the grinder is running, the rule behind the "ground too fine, adjust the mill" message.)
  5. Empty the grounds container and drip tray, reinsert both, and restart so the machine re-homes. Then test with a small handful of beans, not a full hopper.

Two cautions. Do not run the grinder repeatedly against a jam — the motor overheats, turning a clearable blockage into a dead motor. And if the grinder now hums without turning even with the throat clear, the picture has changed: on a Jura the motor has seized and needs replacement; on a De'Longhi a burr is jammed under the top burr, and a service removes and clears it.

Telling a stone from other grinder faults

Not every grinder stoppage is a stone. Philips Error 01 is ground coffee compacted in the funnel between the grinder and the brew group — oily beans ground too fine — and it is cleared with a spoon handle and a vacuum, not surgery. And De'Longhi's "ground too fine" message is not a grinder jam at all: it is brew pressure, and on any machine older than about a year the real fix is usually a descale, not the dial.

When the burrs really are damaged

If the machine ran with the stone in place, the burr edges may be chipped. The signs afterwards are a grinder that is suddenly louder, a grind that drifts inconsistent, and visible nicks on the burr teeth when you inspect them with a light. Damaged burrs are replaceable: a burr set runs about €25 to €45, a Jura grinder motor €60 to €140, and a De'Longhi grinder assembly €60 to €100. On a GIGA, though, burr and grinder-motor replacement means significant teardown — it is a bench repair unless you already service these machines. It is generally worth doing on GIGA and Z machines; on a ten-year-old entry machine, weigh the quote.

Prevention is unglamorous and effective: pour beans into the tray or hopper and give them a two-second look before every refill, and be extra careful with ungraded lots and home-roasted beans.
